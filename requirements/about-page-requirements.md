# About Page — Requirements Document

**Evryday Archive Co**
_Internal reference. For use in development and design implementation._
_Last updated: March 2026_

---

## Purpose & Design Philosophy

The about page has one job: turn a curious visitor into someone who trusts Reed enough to reach out. It does this by telling a story, not presenting a resume.

The page leans deliberately **archival** in its design language — distinct from the portfolio page (which is gallery-forward) and the home page (which blends both instincts). Here, images function as documents. Text functions as reflection. The overall feeling should be: you are looking through someone's archive, not scrolling a photographer's website.

**Design language to carry throughout:**

- Warm off-white background (#F3EDE2)
- Near-black type (#2B2B2B)
- Orange accent (#F06F42) used sparingly
- Plus Jakarta Sans for headings and body
- Archival metadata styling: name, date, location tags on images
- Subtle film grain texture on section backgrounds where appropriate
- Photo corner details on framed images
- No decorative flourishes — restraint is the aesthetic

---

## Narrative Spine

The page tells one connected story in movements. Understanding the arc is more important than any individual section.

1. **The observation** — Reed asked people what they'd hire a photographer for. The answers were thin. A handful of days out of a whole life. That felt like a shame.
2. **Who you are** — Reed has spent years documenting everything, not because he was building a business, but because that's how he sees the world. The breadth is the point.
3. **The archive** — Personal images from Reed's own life. Evidence that this belief has been lived, not just stated.
4. **The decision** — Two walls stand between people and this experience: they don't think their everyday life deserves this kind of attention, and price confirms that doubt. The business exists to push back on both.
5. **The pride** — Honest reflection on the joy of the work done so far and genuine excitement about what's coming.
6. **The invitation** — Bring whatever lights you up. This is an open door.

Sections 1, 2, 4, and 7 are one continuous story told in movements. Sections 3 and 6 are the emotional anchors — one visual, one reflective.

## Section Breakdown

---

### Section 1 — The Observation

**Purpose:** Establish the tension the whole brand is built on. The hook. No bio, no credentials — just the thing that made this worth starting.

**The story beat:** Reed asked the people in his life what they'd consider hiring a photographer for. The answers were consistent: weddings, engagements, grad photos. The big occasions. If you think about a whole life, that's a handful of days out of tens of thousands. That gap is where the brand lives.

**Layout:** Full-width. Large type. Minimal. The statement lives on its own with no competing image. Generous whitespace above and below.

**Copy direction:** Open with the question Reed asked, or the answer he kept hearing. Land on: everyone deserves great photos of more of their life than just the big days. Keep it plain. No flourish.

**Copy (draft — iterate from here):**

> I asked the people in my life what they'd consider hiring a photographer for. Weddings came up. Grad photos. Engagements maybe. That was usually the full list.
>
> If you think about a whole life, that's a handful of days out of tens of thousands.
>
> I started Evryday Archive Co because I think everyone deserves great photos of more of their life than that.

**Design notes:**

- Subtle parallax texture on background — film grain or paper grain
- Orange accent (#F06F42) could punctuate a single word or phrase, not the whole statement
- No image in this section. Let the words land first.

---

### Section 2 — Who I Am as a Photographer

**Purpose:** Reed's relationship with photography is the story here. Not career history, not credentials — the why behind the craft. Someone should come away from this section thinking: he can probably shoot whatever I need, he's got range, and he genuinely cares about it.

**The story beat:** Photography has been a constant throughout Reed's life across many phases and many subjects. Sports, parties, travel, portraits, events, animals, street — not because of any one genre, but because of a deep instinct to document. To say: this is what now looks like. One honest line acknowledges that this has been building for a while and that photography sits alongside other things Reed cares about — enough to signal this isn't a side hustle born from nowhere, not so much that it becomes a bio.

**Layout:** Text-dominant with one candid image of Reed — in the field, not posed. Archival timestamp or location tag overlaid on the image in small type.

**Copy direction:** Lead with the breadth. Name the types of shooting. Then land on the throughline — it's not genre, it's documentation. The gift-for-tomorrow idea belongs here. One grounded line about the fact that this has been building and that photography is one of several passions being pursued intentionally.

**Copy (draft — iterate from here):**

> I shoot portraits and I shoot sports. I shoot events and street and animals and the inside of a room when the light is doing something interesting. I've been the party photographer and the travel photographer and the guy who just always has something nearby to capture with.
>
> The throughline has never been a subject. It's the instinct to document. To say: this is what now looks like. A photo is a gift you leave for your future self, with no idea yet of where you'll be when you find it.
>
> I left a career in software development because I wanted to spend more of my life doing things that actually light me up. Photography has always been one of those things. This is me taking that seriously.

**Design notes:**

- Image: candid of Reed shooting or behind the scenes on a session
- Archival timestamp or location tag on the image in small muted type
- This section can breathe — the longer read is fine here, this is where trust is built

---

### Section 3 — The Archive

**Purpose:** Show, don't tell. Personal images from Reed's own life serving as evidence that this belief has been lived for years, not invented for a business. The images make the argument. The copy pairs with them quietly.

**The story beat:** Reed has been documenting his own everyday life for years across every phase and place. The images here come from that personal archive. Each one is an ordinary moment that turns out to matter.

**Layout:** Image-dominant. A horizontal scroll or paired image-and-text treatment where each copy line is clearly linked to a specific image. The copy is not caption writing — it's short, reflective lines that name the feeling or the idea the image carries.

**Copy direction:** Short lines only. Each one paired intentionally with its image. Together they build the argument that ordinary moments are worth keeping. Copy is written first; images are selected to match after.

**Copy lines (placeholders — pair with images once selected):**

- _The places you lived when you were figuring things out._
- _The people who were there for the in-between._
- _The trip that changed how you thought about things._
- _The animal that made a house feel like home._
- _The version of you that existed before you knew what came next._

**Design notes:**

- Images sourced from Reed's personal archive, not client sessions
- Horizontal scroll on desktop, swipe carousel on mobile
- Each image framed with photo corner details and a small archival metadata tag (location, year — nothing more)
- Copy line style: small, JetBrains Mono, muted — the one place on this page where the archival mono font earns its place
- Copy line and image should feel deliberately paired, not randomly matched
- Images TBD — build the copy lines first, then find images that fit

---

### Section 4 — Why This Exists

**Purpose:** The pivot moment. Connect Reed's personal belief in documentation to the specific reason Evryday Archive Co exists the way it does. Two walls, not one.

**The story beat:** Once Reed started seeing the framework — that people weren't documenting their everyday lives — he started asking why. Two things kept coming up. First: people haven't been given permission to think their everyday life is worth this kind of attention. Milestones feel justified. Tuesdays don't. Second: price confirms that doubt. Photography feels expensive, and expensive things get saved for special occasions. The two barriers reinforce each other. The business pushes back on both at once — by making the case that the everyday is worth it, and by keeping the price somewhere it doesn't shut the conversation down before it starts.

**Layout:** Text-forward. No image needed. Sits as a clear beat change after the archive section. Visually minimal — a thin orange rule or generous spacing to signal the shift.

**Copy direction:** Name both barriers plainly. Make clear the business is a response to both. Keep it grounded — this is a values statement, not a manifesto.

**Copy (draft — iterate from here):**

> When I started looking at why people only book photographers for the big occasions, two things kept coming up.
>
> One is that nobody's told them their everyday life deserves it. Milestones feel justified. The rest of it doesn't. That's something worth pushing back on.
>
> The other is price. Photography feels expensive, and expensive things get saved for special days. Which just confirms the first problem.
>
> Evryday Archive Co exists to do something about both of those things. Accessible pricing so that booking a session can always feel within reach. And a genuine belief that the ordinary parts of your life are worth just as much attention as the extraordinary ones.

**Design notes:**

- No image required
- Thin orange horizontal rule above the section or generous padding to mark the beat change
- This section is the philosophical grounding — design should support the text, not compete with it

### Section 5 — How It Works

**Purpose:** Remove friction for first-time clients. Make the process feel predictable and low-pressure. Hidden behind a click or collapsed by default — the page is full above this point and this information is available elsewhere on the site.

**Implementation note:** Consider a "How does a session work?" accordion, drawer, or modal trigger. Keeps the narrative flow of the about page intact while still making process information available for people who want it before reaching out.

**Steps:**

1. Start with a package — pick one that feels close, we'll adjust from there
2. Show up as you are — no posing you into something that doesn't feel like you
3. Get your preview gallery — browse what we made together
4. Choose what you want to keep — add more if something catches your eye ($7/image)
5. Receive your edited gallery — yours to keep

**Footer note:**

> Pricing is on the site. No surprises, no hidden fees.

**Design notes:**

- Triggered by a small text link or button, not a prominent section header
- Orange accent dot as divider between steps, consistent with existing Instagram design language
- Calm and light — functional, not emotional

---

### Section 6 — The Work So Far

**Purpose:** Honest pride in what's been made and genuine excitement about what's coming. Not a stats showcase. A person reflecting on a few months of meaningful work.

**The story beat:** The sessions done since starting Evryday Archive Co have produced images that clients genuinely love. That matters. It's early but it's real, and there's a lot more to look forward to.

**Layout:** One strong client session image, large format. Copy below or alongside, restrained. Feels like a caption on an archival print.

**Copy (draft — iterate from here):**

> A few months in, I've had the chance to make images for people that they actually love. That doesn't get old.
>
> I'm proud of what's come out of these early sessions. And honestly I'm just getting started.

**Design notes:**

- One image, full-bleed or large format — high quality, emotionally resonant, from a real client session
- No stats, no reach numbers — the image and the honesty do the work
- Archival metadata tag on the image: client first name, date

---

### Section 7 — The Invitation

**Purpose:** Open door. Low pressure. The page ends as a conversation starting, not a sale closing.

**The story beat:** Sessions are built around what lights people up — everyday activities, hobbies, jobs, passions, people, pets, sports, whatever it is. Reed is always up for something creative and genuinely wants to make great images together. There's no wrong reason to reach out.

**Layout:** Stripped back. Wide margins, generous whitespace. Text only. CTA in brand style.

**Copy direction:** Lead with the range of what a session can be. Land on the open door. Make it feel like the start of a conversation, not the end of a pitch.

**Copy (draft — iterate from here):**

> Sessions are built around what matters to you. Your job, your hobby, your people, your pet, the thing you do every weekend that nobody has ever thought to photograph. If it lights you up, I want to shoot it.
>
> I'm always up for something creative, and I'd rather make something genuinely good together than tick a box.
>
> If you've been thinking about it, reach out. No commitment in asking.

**CTA:** `Get in touch` — links to contact/booking flow

**Design notes:**

- No image. The page has earned this quiet ending.
- CTA button: brand orange (#F06F42), restrained size
- Thin orange horizontal rule above the section as a visual close

## Interaction & Motion Notes

- **Parallax texture** on opening section background (subtle, not distracting)
- **Horizontal scroll** on Section 3 image gallery (desktop); swipe carousel (mobile)
- **Section 5 collapsed by default** — triggered by a text link or small button, not a visible section header
- **No autoplay, no aggressive animation** — motion should feel like turning a page, not a product demo
- **Hover state on archival captions** — caption detail fades in gently on image hover
- All interactions should feel **slow and deliberate**, consistent with the archival mood

## Voice Reminders for Copy Review

- No em dashes
- No "not X, but Y" constructions
- No inspirational phrasing or moral-of-the-story endings
- Avoid symmetrical sentence structures
- Let imperfection exist — don't over-polish
- First person singular throughout (I, not we — this is Reed's page)
- Short sentences are fine. So are longer ones when the thought earns it.
- The LinkedIn intro post covers the career history well — this page does not need to retread that ground
